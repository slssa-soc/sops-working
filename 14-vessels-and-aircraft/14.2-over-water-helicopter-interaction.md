## Purpose

To outline the procedure for SLS services interacting with a helicopter during over-water operations.

## Overview

Helicopter operations over water require clear communication, safe watercraft positioning, and coordination between SLS personnel and aircraft crew.

Watercraft may be required to support winch operations, patient transfer, search and rescue activity, or other operational tasks.

The aircraft crew has operational control of the aircraft and associated winch or aviation activity. SLS watercraft must follow aircraft crew directions where safe to do so.

## Procedure

### Communications

All watercraft should maintain communications with the aircraft crew on the nominated radio channel where possible.

Where radio communications are not available or are ineffective, watercraft operators should watch for aircraft crew hand signals.

Radio communications should be clear, concise, and limited to operationally necessary information.

### Watercraft Positioning

When operating with a helicopter over water, watercraft should generally be positioned at the 2 o’clock position relative to the helicopter, unless otherwise directed by the aircraft crew.

Watercraft operators must maintain safe distance, avoid rotor wash hazards where possible, and remain aware of aircraft movement, winch activity, swimmers, patients, other vessels, and environmental conditions.

### RWC Operations

Where safe to do so, an RWC should face away from the incoming helicopter so that the rescue sled is exposed and available for an unobstructed winch operation.

The RWC operator should maintain position and await direction from the aircraft crew.

### IRB Operations

An IRB driver should attempt to face the incoming helicopter and position the IRB so the helicopter approaches from the 2 o’clock angle.

The IRB driver must maintain situational awareness and be prepared to reposition if directed by the aircraft crew or if conditions change.

### Safety Considerations

Personnel involved in over-water helicopter interaction should consider:

- Rotor wash,
- Reduced visibility caused by spray,
- Noise and communication limitations,
- Patient security,
- Loose equipment,
- Vessel stability,
- Swimmers or other persons in the water,
- Proximity to other vessels or hazards,
- Fatigue and crew readiness, and
- Changing wind, swell, current, and surf conditions.

Watercraft must not move under or into the aircraft operating area unless directed by the aircraft crew and safe to do so.
