## Purpose

To outline the information factors that should be collected, assessed, recorded, and communicated during search and rescue (SAR) operations involving SLSSA personnel, services, and assets.

## Overview

Accurate and timely information is critical to effective SAR operations.

SAR information supports:

- Initial assessment,
- Control Agency notification,
- Search urgency assessment,
- Search planning,
- Resource tasking,
- Risk assessment,
- Search pattern selection,
- Incident logging,
- Briefings,
- SITREPs,
- Transfer of command or coordination, and
- Conclusion or suspension decisions.

The National Search and Rescue Manual (NATSAR Manual) provides nationally agreed SAR doctrine, terminology, planning principles, and operational guidance for Australian SAR authorities and organisations that provide SAR services.

In South Australia, South Australia Police (SAPOL) is the control agency for all search and rescue activities.

SLSSA personnel must collect and communicate relevant SAR information as soon as reasonably practicable, while continuing to prioritise immediate life-saving action and member safety.

## Definitions

**Last Known Position (LKP)** means the last confirmed location where the missing person, vessel, craft, object, or search target was seen or known to be.

**Point Last Seen (PLS)** means the location where the missing person, vessel, craft, object, or search target was last observed by a witness.

**Search Object** means the person, vessel, craft, object, or item being searched for.

**Informant** means the person who initially reports the incident or provides relevant information.

**Witness** means a person who saw, heard, or otherwise has direct information relevant to the SAR incident.

**SITREP** means Situation Report.

## Procedure

### Information Collection

SLS personnel should collect SAR information as early as possible.

Information should be:

- Accurate,
- Factual,
- Timely,
- Clearly communicated,
- Recorded where practicable,
- Updated as new information becomes available, and
- Provided to the State Operations Centre (SOC).

Information collection must not delay immediate rescue action, urgent escalation, or emergency service notification.

Where information is incomplete or uncertain, this should be clearly stated.

### Initial Information

Initial information should be provided to the SOC as soon as reasonably practicable.

Initial information should include:

- Incident location,
- Nature of the incident,
- Whether a person is missing, overdue, in distress, or believed to be in the water,
- Number of persons involved,
- Last known position or point last seen,
- Time last seen,
- Who reported the incident,
- Whether emergency services have been contacted,
- Immediate actions already taken,
- Available SLS resources,
- Environmental conditions,
- Known hazards, and
- Any urgent support required.

Where there is any doubt about whether the incident is a SAR incident, personnel should notify the SOC and seek direction.

### Search Object Information

Information about the search object should be collected as soon as practicable.

For a missing person, this may include:

- Name,
- Age,
- Sex,
- Height and build,
- Clothing description,
- Swimming ability,
- Medical conditions,
- Behavioural or cognitive factors,
- Level of distress,
- Fitness,
- Language or communication barriers,
- Whether they are familiar with the location,
- Whether they are alone or with others,
- Whether they have flotation or safety equipment,
- Whether they have a mobile phone or communication device,
- Whether they have identification, and
- Any known intentions or likely destination.

For a vessel, craft, board, ski, kayak, or other object, this may include:

- Type,
- Size,
- Colour,
- Registration or markings,
- Number of persons on board,
- Safety equipment,
- Communications equipment,
- Direction of travel,
- Last known route,
- Intended destination,
- Fuel or battery limitations,
- Condition when last seen, and
- Any identifying features.

### Location Information

Location information should be recorded as clearly as possible.

This may include:

- Beach or location name,
- Nearest access point,
- Street address or cross street,
- GPS coordinates where available,
- Distance and direction from known landmarks,
- Patrol area reference,
- Flagged area reference,
- Rock, reef, jetty, ramp, river mouth, or headland reference,
- Water depth where known,
- Distance offshore,
- Direction of travel, and
- Any access or egress limitations.

Where GPS coordinates are used, personnel should confirm the format if possible and provide the information exactly as displayed.

### Time Information

Time information is critical to SAR planning.

Personnel should record:

- Time of initial report,
- Time last seen,
- Time the person entered the water or departed,
- Time the person was expected to return,
- Time emergency services were notified,
- Time SOC was notified,
- Time SLS resources were deployed,
- Time search areas were checked,
- Time significant changes occurred, and
- Time the person or object was located, if applicable.

Where times are estimated, this should be clearly stated.

### Witness and Informant Information

Witnesses and informants should be identified and kept available where practicable.

Information should include:

- Name,
- Contact number,
- Location,
- Relationship to the missing person or incident,
- What they saw or were told,
- When they last saw the person or object,
- Where they last saw the person or object,
- Direction of travel,
- Any changes in behaviour or condition,
- Any photos, videos, messages, tracking information, or other evidence, and
- Whether police or emergency services may need to contact them.

Witnesses should be encouraged to remain available and monitor their phone.

### Environmental Information

Environmental information assists search planning, risk assessment, and drift estimation.

Relevant environmental information may include:

- Surf conditions,
- Swell size and direction,
- Wind speed and direction,
- Tide,
- Current,
- Sweep,
- Rip currents,
- Water temperature,
- Visibility,
- Weather,
- Rain,
- Lightning,
- Fog,
- Sun glare,
- Sea state,
- Water colour or turbidity,
- Debris,
- Marine creatures,
- Pollution or contamination,
- Rock, reef, cliff, or terrain hazards, and
- Changes expected during the operation.

Environmental conditions should be updated where they change or where the SAR operation continues for an extended period.

### Search Area Information

Personnel should record and communicate search area information.

This may include:

- Areas already checked,
- Areas not yet checked,
- Search direction,
- Search pattern used,
- Start and finish points,
- Search track or GPS track where available,
- Search duration,
- Search speed,
- Spacing between searchers or assets,
- Visibility limitations,
- Areas of high probability,
- Hazards encountered,
- Areas unable to be searched,
- Reasons areas were not searched, and
- Any sightings or possible clues.

Where multiple SLS resources are involved, the SOC should be updated regularly on search areas covered and areas remaining.

### Resource Information

Resource information assists the SOC, Duty Officer, State Duty Officer, and Control Agency to understand available capability.

Information may include:

- SLS personnel available,
- Qualifications and capability,
- Patrol status,
- Lifeguard availability,
- ERT availability,
- IRB availability,
- RWC availability,
- JRB availability,
- RPAS availability,
- CSCN availability,
- Vehicle availability,
- First aid and emergency care capability,
- Communications capability,
- Equipment limitations,
- Estimated response times,
- Fatigue considerations, and
- Any constraints on deployment.

Resources must not be represented as available where they are not crewed, equipped, operational, or within approved capability.

### Communications Information

Communications arrangements should be confirmed and communicated.

This may include:

- Primary radio channel,
- Secondary radio channel,
- Phone contact numbers,
- SOC contact method,
- Emergency services contact point,
- SLS command contact,
- Control Agency contact,
- SITREP frequency,
- Communications black spots,
- Call signs,
- Backup communications method, and
- Any limitations.

Where communications are unreliable, this must be escalated and considered in task acceptance and risk assessment.

### Operational Risk Information

Operational risk information should be collected and communicated throughout the SAR operation.

This may include:

- Hazards to SLS personnel,
- Hazards to the public,
- Hazards to the missing person or patient,
- Weather or water conditions,
- Access and egress concerns,
- Fatigue,
- Reduced visibility,
- Night operations,
- Vessel traffic,
- Aircraft operations,
- Equipment limitations,
- Communications limitations,
- Public behaviour,
- Scene safety,
- Evidence or scene preservation issues, and
- Whether specialist resources are required.

Risk information should be considered in accordance with [SOP 11.3 – Risk vs Gain](../11-search-and-rescue-sar-operations/11.3-risk-vs-gain.md).

### Information Assessment

Information should be assessed for reliability and relevance.

Personnel should consider:

- Whether the information is confirmed or unconfirmed,
- Whether the source is direct or indirect,
- Whether information conflicts with other information,
- Whether the time is accurate or estimated,
- Whether the location is precise or approximate,
- Whether witness accounts are consistent,
- Whether the information changes the search area or priority,
- Whether the information changes the risk profile, and
- Whether the information should be escalated immediately.

Unconfirmed information may still be operationally useful, but it must be clearly identified as unconfirmed.

### Information Updates

SAR information will often change during an operation.

The SOC should be updated when:

- New information is received,
- Information is corrected,
- The search object is located,
- The last known position changes,
- Search areas are completed,
- Search areas cannot be completed,
- Conditions change,
- Resources are deployed or stood down,
- Risks change,
- Witness information changes,
- Emergency services arrive or assume control,
- Tasking changes, or
- SLS personnel withdraw or require support.

Updates should be concise, factual, and provided through the agreed communications pathway.

### Recording Information

SAR information must be recorded where practicable.

Records may include:

- Incident logs,
- Operations App records,
- SOC incident records,
- Contemporaneous notes,
- Search maps,
- GPS tracks,
- Photos or videos,
- Witness details,
- SITREPs,
- Tasking records,
- Search area records,
- Resource deployment records,
- Decisions and approvals, and
- Follow-up actions.

Records should be provided to the SOC and managed in accordance with incident recording requirements.

### Information Sharing

SAR information should be shared only with personnel or agencies who require it for operational, safety, welfare, legal, or reporting purposes.

Information may need to be shared with:

- SOC,
- Duty Officer,
- State Duty Officer,
- Lifesaving Department,
- SAPOL or other Control Agency,
- Emergency services,
- SLS services involved in the response,
- Incident Controller,
- Authorised support agencies, and
- Authorised SLSSA personnel.

Information involving personal details, patient details, images, video, witness details, or sensitive incident information must be managed appropriately.

### Briefings and SITREPs

SAR information should support clear briefings and SITREPs.

Briefings should provide sufficient information for personnel to understand:

- The situation,
- The task,
- The search object,
- The search area,
- Known hazards,
- Available resources,
- Communications arrangements,
- Safety requirements,
- Reporting requirements, and
- Any limitations.

Further guidance is provided in [SOP 11.5 – SAR Briefings - SMEACS](../11-search-and-rescue-sar-operations/11.5-sar-briefings-smeacs.md).

### Transfer of Command or Coordination

When command or coordination is transferred, relevant SAR information must be passed to the incoming role.

This should include:

- Current incident status,
- Search object information,
- Last known position,
- Actions completed,
- Search areas covered,
- Resources deployed,
- Current tasking,
- Known hazards,
- Communications arrangements,
- Control Agency contact,
- Records completed,
- Outstanding actions, and
- Any limitations or concerns.

Further guidance is provided in [SOP 11.10 – Information Exchange in Transfer of Command](../11-search-and-rescue-sar-operations/11.10-information-exchange-in-transfer-of-command.md).

### Minimum SAR Information Prompt

Where time permits, SLS personnel should attempt to collect the following minimum information:

| **Information Factor** | **Details to Collect** |
| --- | --- |
| Incident | What has happened or what is suspected? |
| Search object | Who or what is missing, overdue, or in distress? |
| Last known position | Where was the person, vessel, craft, or object last seen or known to be? |
| Time | When were they last seen or expected back? |
| Direction | Which way were they moving or likely to move? |
| Description | What do they look like? What are they wearing or using? |
| Capability | Swimming ability, medical issues, flotation, communication devices, safety equipment. |
| Witnesses | Who saw them? Are they still available? |
| Conditions | Surf, wind, tide, current, weather, visibility, hazards. |
| Actions taken | What has already been searched, checked, or done? |
| Resources | What SLS resources are available or deployed? |
| Risks | What risks or limitations are present? |
| Support required | What assistance is needed now? |
