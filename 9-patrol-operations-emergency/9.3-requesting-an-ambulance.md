## Purpose

To outline operational procedures and minimum information requirements when requesting ambulance assistance through the State Operations Centre (SOC).

## Overview

Timely and accurate information gathering supports effective ambulance dispatch, patient management, and emergency service coordination.

Operational personnel should prioritise early notification to the State Operations Centre (SOC), concise communications, and ongoing patient condition updates.

The State Operations Centre (SOC) coordinates emergency service notifications and ambulance requests on behalf of SLS services.

## Procedure

Good incident management involves the accurate collection and concise communication of information.

SA Ambulance Service (SAAS) requires specific information to support ambulance dispatch and patient prioritisation. Lifesaving services should align patient information gathering and communications with these requirements.

Ambulance requests should be coordinated through the State Operations Centre (SOC), or if the SOC is not available, via Triple Zero.

### Patient Reporting

Patient information should be communicated to the SOC as soon as reasonably practicable. Requests for ambulance assistance must not be delayed while obtaining complete information.

Additional information and patient condition updates should be provided as they become available.

Operational communications channels should continue to be monitored in accordance with [SOP 7.5 – Zones and Channels](../7-communications/7.5-zones-and-channels.md).

The following information should be obtained where practicable:

1. Patient sex,
2. Approximate age,
3. Approximate weight where relevant,
4. Mechanism of injury or illness,
5. Nature of injuries or symptoms,
6. Breathing status,
7. Level of consciousness,
8. Presence of chest pain where applicable,
9. Exact patient location and best access point,
10. Treatment being provided, and
11. Any significant changes in patient condition.

Additional information that may assist ambulance response includes:

- Is the patient changing colour?
- Is the patient clammy?
- Does the patient have a history of heart problems?
- Has the patient consumed any drugs, alcohol, or medication?

### Emergency Radio Communications

**Remember:** Position, Problem, People, Progress

| **Action** | Explanation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inform SurfCom (via radio)** | Identifies your transmission as an emergency and prioritises it above non-emergency traffic | “**Rescue, Rescue, Rescue** – SurfCom, SurfCom, this is \[Club/Service Name\], over” |
| **Position** | Advise the patient location and best access point for emergency services | “Patient has been transported to the Surf Club, located at the corner of Main Road and Smith Road. A lifesaver will meet the ambulance roadside, over” |
| **Problem** | Describe the mechanism of injury or medical emergency | “SurfCom, we have one patient struck by a surfboard with severe head lacerations” |
| **People** | Provide patient details and condition | “Patient is male, approximately 36 years old. Patient is breathing and conscious. Severe bleeding from the head. No chest pain reported, over” |
| **Progress** | Provide updates regarding patient condition or treatment | “SurfCom, the patient has now lost consciousness, over” |

### Coordination with the State Operations Centre (SOC)

The State Operations Centre (SOC) may:

- Coordinate ambulance dispatch,
- Coordinate emergency service notifications,
- Assist with access arrangements,
- Coordinate helicopter or additional operational resources where required, and
- Maintain operational communications and incident logging.
